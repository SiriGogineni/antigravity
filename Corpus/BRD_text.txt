Executive Control Plane: A Single Autonomous Agent for C-Level Executives

1. Executive Summary
This project delivers one autonomous AI agent, the Executive Agent, that acts as a digital chief of staff for each C-level executive (CEO, CFO, COO, CTO, CMO, CHRO) of a small business enterprise. A lightweight control plane wraps the agent so that everything it does is governed, explainable, reversible and cost-controlled.
There is a single agent per executive, with one reasoning loop, one memory and one set of tools. It is not a fleet of cooperating agents. Capabilities such as briefing, inbox triage, scheduling, meeting preparation, decision support and follow-up tracking are skills of that one agent.
The agent runs on GCP and is built with Python, FastAPI, Google ADK, LangGraph and LangChain. It uses PostgreSQL with pgvector for memory, Langfuse for observability, Vertex AI and Ollama-served Llama models for inference, and a React.js console for oversight. Git versions code, prompts and policies. Google Antigravity is the engineering team's development environment, and the A2A protocol is exposed only as an optional external interface.
2. Business Context and Problem Statement
Executives in small enterprises have no large support staff, so their scarcest resource, attention, is spent on coordination rather than decisions.
Inboxes, calendars, chat and documents are spread across tools; executives spend hours each week triaging and context-switching.
Preparation for meetings, board and investor updates is manual and often late.
Commitments made in meetings and email are lost or tracked inconsistently.
KPI changes and risks are noticed after the fact, because reports are compiled by hand.
Executives will not hand over authority to AI that can send messages, commit money or expose confidential information without tight, visible control.
Problem statement: Executives need a trusted assistant that works autonomously on routine work, protects confidential information, asks before acting on anything consequential, and shows exactly what it did and why.
Why one agent: A single agent keeps one consistent understanding of the executive's priorities, preferences and context. It is simpler to govern, audit, secure and explain than a multi-agent system, and it costs less to run, which suits a small enterprise.

4. Scope
In scope (Release 1)
One Executive Agent instance per executive, each with its own identity, memory, permissions and budget.
Executive capabilities: daily briefing, inbox triage and drafting, scheduling, meeting preparation and follow-up, KPI and risk monitoring, decision-support memos, document and board-pack drafting, commitment tracking, travel and logistics support.
Control plane: policy and autonomy settings, approval inbox, kill switch, audit log, cost budgets, observability, connector management and administration.
Conversational and notification channels: web console, email and chat notifications [confirm: Slack, Teams, SMS].
Integrations: Google Workspace (Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Docs), accounting, CRM and chat [confirm list].
Model gateway across Vertex AI and Ollama-hosted Llama models.
Out of scope (Release 1)
Multiple cooperating agents, agent marketplaces and agent-to-agent delegation inside the platform.
Autonomous payments, contract signature or any irreversible financial commitment.
Voice and telephony, and native mobile apps (responsive web only).
Agents for non-executive staff.
Regulated workflows such as clinical or legal advice [confirm].

6. Solution Overview
The Executive Agent is one autonomous agent with a plan, act, observe loop, durable memory and a registry of tools, supervised by the control plane. The control plane decides what the agent may do; the agent decides how to do it.

14. Acceptance Criteria and Open Questions
Acceptance criteria (Release 1)
An executive onboards, connects Gmail and Calendar, and receives a first correct daily brief within 30 minutes.
All Must requirements pass user acceptance testing.
100% of agent actions appear in the audit log and in Langfuse traces.
Red-team tests show 0 unapproved high-risk actions and 0 successful prompt-injection exfiltrations.
The kill switch halts all activity and revokes tool access within 10 seconds.
Brief accuracy reaches at least 95% on the golden dataset.
Pilot executives report at least 8 hours per week saved and a trust score of 4 out of 5 or higher.
Load, failover and cost-budget tests meet the non-functional targets.
